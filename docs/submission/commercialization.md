## Short version (D7 summary field)

A NASA Moon delivery order costs $50–323 million, and IM-2 reached the south pole in March 2025 only to end early on its side, sending some data before its batteries were depleted. Penumbra stays free and open, and sells sign-off planning reports for custom sites and dates for less than 0.1% of the order they protect.

## Cost of failure

NASA buys Moon deliveries as task orders under CLPS, a programme capped at $2.6 billion through November 2028 [C3]. Order values as of February 2024 [C1]:

- Intuitive Machines IM-1: $122.0 million, up from $81.7 million.
- Intuitive Machines IM-2 (PRIME-1): $50.0 million. It reached the south pole in March 2025, but the lander was on its side and the mission ended early; NASA got only some data before its batteries were depleted [C2].
- Astrobotic Griffin (originally the VIPER delivery): $322.8 million, up from $199.5 million.

The payloads cost more again: VIPER was a $505.4 million rover [C1]. A solar lander has only the hours of sun and Earth contact its site and date give it; Penumbra counts those hours before the order is spent.

## Buyers

- CLPS lander vendors choosing sites and landing dates.
- NASA and partner landing-site and operations teams for the crewed south-pole landing.
- Rover and ISRU teams who need power and comms hours at a traverse stop.
- Universities and educators teaching mission design with real NASA data.

## Model

Open core. The browser app and the Rust model stay free and open source, so anyone can check the numbers against JPL Horizons and LOLA.

The paid part is a mission-planning service: custom sites and dates, higher-resolution DEMs, and a signed sign-off report for a design review. Illustrative price anchor: $25,000 per site-and-date study. That is 0.05% of a $50 million task order.

## Why now

- NASA moved the first crewed south-pole landing to Artemis IV in 2028; Artemis III, in 2027, now tests systems in Earth orbit [C4].
- Blue Origin's Blue Moon MK1 is set to land on the Shackleton connecting ridge in 2027 [C5]. NASA also lists south-pole deliveries for Intuitive Machines (Nova-C to Mons Mouton, Nova-D), Firefly (Blue Ghost 4) and Astrobotic (Griffin to Mons Mouton) [C6].
- China's Chang'e-7 will survey the south pole; in May 2026 its launch was planned for the second half of 2026 [C7].

Every one of these teams has to answer the same question for its site and date: how many hours of sun and Earth contact, and when does the first shadow arrive.

## D4 line
